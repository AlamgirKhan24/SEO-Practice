# Module 7: Off-Page SEO

## Project context

- **Practice business:** GlowCare Salon (fictional)
- **Target area:** University Town, Peshawar, Pakistan
- **Website status:** No live site or real brand presence yet. Competitor backlink counts and GlowCare's own links cannot be measured until a real site exists.
- **Purpose:** Earn genuine visibility and relevant mentions by creating something useful and building real community relationships. A high backlink count by itself is not a success measure.

## Checklist

- [ ] Once a site is live and verified, review competitor links with a free backlink tool. Record the tool, date, and its coverage limits; competitor data is an estimate, not a complete link inventory.
- [ ] List legitimate business directories and citations relevant to the actual market. Add accurate information consistently; skip low-quality directories created mainly to sell links.
- [ ] Consider contributing an expert article only when a reputable publisher's audience would benefit from it. Do not use guest posts primarily as a link scheme. Any paid or sponsored link should be properly qualified by the publisher (for example, `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`).
- [ ] Look for broken links or resource pages only where a genuinely useful, relevant replacement exists. Contact publishers politely; do not send bulk or misleading pitches.
- [ ] Build local relationships with real suppliers, community groups, event organizers, or publications. A mention or link should be editorially earned, not required as a condition of an unrelated deal.
- [ ] Create a useful asset only if it helps customers, such as an honest bridal appointment checklist or local wedding planning guide. Do not create thin content just to attract links.
- [ ] Share useful content on appropriate social channels. Social posts can bring visitors and awareness; do not count ordinary social posts as backlinks.
- [ ] Monitor genuine brand mentions after the business has a real name and website. Ask for a link only when it helps readers and the publisher chooses freely.
- [ ] Answer relevant community questions with useful, transparent replies. Avoid promotional spam, repeated links, and keyword-rich forum signatures.
- [ ] Review new links periodically. Investigate clearly manipulative link patterns; do not disavow links solely because a third-party tool labels them low quality.

## Draft outreach email (review and personalize before sending)

**Subject:** Possible resource for your [page/topic]

Hi [Name],

I found your [specific page] while researching [topic] and noticed that [brief, accurate observation]. We have a [specific useful resource] that may help your readers: [URL]. If you think it fits, feel free to include it. No worries if it is not a match.

Thanks,
[Your name]

For broken-link outreach, confirm that the URL is actually broken and that your page is a genuinely suitable replacement. Do not claim your content replaces a resource if it does not.

## Free tools

- Google Search Console Links report (for a verified site; not a complete web link index)
- Ahrefs Webmaster Tools (for sites you can verify; competitor access may be limited)
- Ubersuggest free tier (limited estimates)
- Google Alerts or manual search for brand mentions
- Google Sheets for the tracker
- Canva for creating a useful visual asset, if one is needed

## Final report evidence

- Completed link tracker with the linking page, destination page, date found, and evidence that each link is live.
- Backlinks and referring domains before and after, naming the source tool and noting its limitations.
- Outreach count and reply rate only if outreach was actually conducted.
- Referral traffic from earned links, where analytics can attribute it.
- Screenshots or saved URLs for the most relevant earned mentions.
- Content or local partnerships that produced real audience value, even if no link resulted.
- Any sponsored links clearly identified and properly qualified.

## Policy note

Google defines link spam as creating links primarily to manipulate rankings. Its examples include paid ranking links, excessive exchanges, automated link creation, low-quality directory links, and guest-post campaigns whose main purpose is links. Focus on useful content and real editorial relationships. See [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) and [Google's guidance on link qualification](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links).
