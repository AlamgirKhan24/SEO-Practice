# Target Audience Research

## Research status

These are five **synthetic planning personas**, derived from the project brief and public market context. They are not survey respondents and demographic details such as age, income, and location are intentionally not assigned without reliable project-specific evidence. Validate with interviews, support questions, onsite search, Search Console, and conversion behavior after launch.

## Audience segments

| Priority | Segment | Primary job to be done | Typical purchase trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Students and university students | Choose an affordable, portable laptop for coursework and daily use | New semester, old device failure, software requirement |
| Primary | Professionals and freelancers | Select a reliable device for work, meetings, and productivity | New role/client, replacement cycle, performance bottleneck |
| Primary | Gamers and creators | Match GPU/CPU/display/cooling capability to games or creative workloads | New title/software, upgrade, budget availability |
| Secondary | Technology enthusiasts | Compare models/specifications and buy a desired configuration | Product launch, deal, component or device upgrade |
| Secondary | Small business/education buyers | Standardize devices and confirm volume availability/support | Hiring, procurement cycle, refresh or expansion |

## Persona cards

### 1. Ayesha — student value seeker (archetype)
- **Need:** dependable everyday use, portability, battery, storage, and an affordable total price.
- **Concerns:** confusing CPU generations, low-quality listings, unexpected delivery charges, and warranty ambiguity.
- **Decision path:** shortlist by budget/use; compare specifications; ask family/peers; verify return and warranty details.
- **Content/product support:** plain-language spec glossary, student use-case category only if enough relevant inventory, comparison table, transparent total price and delivery.
- **Do not assume:** exact budget, age, gender-based behavior, or that every student needs the same configuration.

### 2. Hamza — early-career professional/freelancer (archetype)
- **Need:** responsive multitasking, video-call reliability, portability, and confidence that work can continue if support is needed.
- **Concerns:** downtime, weak battery, uncertain stock, slow delivery, and unclear service escalation.
- **Decision path:** filters by workload and portability; checks model details and warranty; seeks fast presale confirmation.
- **Support:** workload-based guides, accurate SKU data, realistic delivery promise, visible support/return policy.

### 3. Sana — gaming buyer balancing performance and price (archetype)
- **Need:** GPU/CPU/display/cooling combination appropriate to target games and budget.
- **Concerns:** misleading “gaming” labels, thermals, upgrade limitations, imported variant differences, and warranty coverage.
- **Decision path:** compares GPU tier, display, thermals/reviews, stock and warranty; may wait for a specific model or promotion.
- **Support:** verified exact model/configuration, clear component specs, buyer guide with methodology, live availability.

### 4. Bilal — creator and power user (archetype)
- **Need:** enough CPU/GPU/RAM, color-appropriate display, storage, ports, and workflow fit.
- **Concerns:** spec omissions, mismatched marketing claims, lack of upgrade/service information.
- **Decision path:** starts with software/workload; checks full specifications, display and ports; compares value across configurations.
- **Support:** structured complete spec tables, use-case guidance reviewed by knowledgeable authors, clear variant naming.

### 5. Procurement lead — small organization or institution (archetype)
- **Need:** repeatable model choice, quantity availability, documentation, delivery coordination, and service terms.
- **Concerns:** stock inconsistency, changing quotes, invoicing/payment process, and support at scale.
- **Decision path:** confirms need and budget; requests a quote; checks supplier credibility and delivery/service terms.
- **Support:** only add a B2B quote path if operationally supported; provide contact and procurement details; do not promise volume service prematurely.

## Shared purchase considerations (hypotheses to validate)

For a high-consideration electronics purchase, likely decision criteria include total price, exact model/configuration, stock status, warranty and return terms, delivery coverage/time, payment options, seller credibility, reviews, performance fit, and access to support. The relative importance of these factors has not been measured for TechFind. Validate through interviews, customer-service records (if any), structured onsite feedback, and analytics.

## Research plan for validation

1. Conduct 8–12 short interviews across the primary groups as a qualitative discovery sample; report this as exploratory, not representative.
2. Ask about most recent laptop purchase, selection criteria, abandoned options, trust signals, language, and service expectations.
3. Review de-identified presale questions and return reasons, if an operating business exists.
4. After launch, compare search queries, landing-page engagement, product views, add-to-cart, and purchase completion by page/use case.
5. Update personas when evidence changes; keep assumptions labeled and avoid collecting unnecessary personal data.
