# TechFind Content SEO and Editorial Guidelines

**Status:** Proposed operating guide; adapt to verified business details and current source documentation before publishing.

## Plan for people and real tasks

- Start each assignment with the reader's decision or problem, not a keyword list.
- Use Module 03 keywords as hypotheses. Search volume and difficulty were unavailable; verify demand/intent before committing significant production time.
- Add firsthand expertise, original comparison, useful explanation, or verified catalog information. Do not paraphrase competitor pages to create a thin substitute.
- State limitations, update dates and source methods where they help a buyer assess the advice.

## Keyword placement and page boundaries

- Give each page one primary intent and primary keyword target, as mapped in Module 03.
- Use the topic naturally in the title, H1 and opening when it fits. Use related phrases only where they help explain the subject.
- Avoid repeating exact phrases, awkward variants, or city/budget modifiers solely for search engines.
- Keep category pages focused on browsing and availability; use guides to answer questions; reserve exact model/configuration terms for real product pages.
- Avoid separate near-identical pages for every budget, brand, city or filter. Confirm distinct inventory and reader value before creating a landing page.

## Structure and readability

- Use one clear H1, followed by meaningful H2 sections and nested H3s where needed.
- Put the direct answer and decision-critical information early. Use concise paragraphs, descriptive lists and tables for comparisons.
- Explain technical terms in plain language; do not assume every reader knows processor, memory, storage or port terminology.
- Make tables usable on mobile and screen readers. Avoid using headings for styling alone.

## Factual accuracy and E-E-A-T signals

- Verify specifications against manufacturer documentation for the exact model/variant; record source and date.
- Verify prices, stock, condition, included items, warranty, delivery and return facts against current business records and approved policy.
- Identify authors/reviewers accurately. Do not invent credentials, testing, customer feedback, ownership history or first-hand experience.
- For troubleshooting, give safe, proportionate steps and clear stop conditions. Send readers to the manufacturer or qualified technician when appropriate.
- Distinguish manufacturer claims from TechFind observations and seller policy. Disclose comparison methodology where applicable.

## Images and accessibility

- Use real product imagery that depicts the exact model/configuration and viewpoint. Do not substitute generic images without labeling them clearly.
- Use concise descriptive filenames and alt text that conveys the visible information. Decorative images should have empty alt text.
- Provide responsive sizes/formats, explicit dimensions, and appropriate loading behavior; do not lazy-load a likely main above-the-fold image.
- Preserve readable contrast, keyboard access, visible focus, labeled controls and text alternatives for important visual information.

## Internal and external links

- Link contextually to relevant categories, exact products, guides and verified policy pages using descriptive natural anchors.
- Use only live, useful destinations. Check links during editorial QA and update links when a product/page changes.
- Cite primary sources for technical requirements, safety advice, policy/legal requirements and product specifications. Keep a source log with title, URL, publisher, accessed date and the claim supported.
- Do not link to sources solely to manipulate authority, and do not present a source as supporting a claim it does not contain.

## Freshness and review

- Set a review cadence based on volatility: product/price/policy content should be checked when the underlying data changes; evergreen explainers should have a scheduled review.
- Update only when facts or usefulness materially change. Record what changed and why; do not change dates without substantive review.
- Retire or consolidate outdated pages only after checking traffic, links, user need, canonical and redirect implications with the technical owner.

## Final editorial checklist

- Does the page satisfy a clearly stated reader need?
- Is every price, specification, availability and policy fact traceable to a current source?
- Is the page distinct from existing pages and assigned to one primary intent?
- Does it use clear headings and readable, accessible formatting?
- Are internal links relevant and working?
- Are external references authoritative and actually supportive?
- Is a proposed CTA accurate for what TechFind currently offers?
- Has a knowledgeable editor reviewed high-impact technical or safety advice?
