Sunscreen packaging can include terms such as SPF, broad spectrum and water resistance. These terms describe labeled product attributes, but shoppers should read the full package and use a product according to its directions. This guide explains a few terms without ranking products or promising a particular outcome.

## What SPF indicates
SPF is a labeled measure associated with protection from sunburn under specified test conditions. It should not be read as a measure of every kind of sun exposure or as permission to stay in the sun indefinitely. Follow the label and public-health recommendations.

## What broad spectrum means
In the United States, FDA guidance describes “broad spectrum” labeling in terms of protection against both UVA and UVB radiation under its testing requirements. Regulatory definitions vary across markets, so GlowCare should explain the label applicable to the product and avoid implying identical rules worldwide.

## Water-resistance wording
Some products carry a time-limited water-resistance statement under the relevant jurisdiction’s labeling system. That does not mean a product is waterproof or sweatproof. Check the exact package for duration and reapplication instructions.

## Follow the product directions
Use the labeled amount and application instructions. Reapply as directed, especially after activities identified on the label. Sunscreen is one part of sun protection; shade and clothing can also matter. Consult local health guidance for current recommendations.

## Questions to ask when shopping
- Is the product name and size clear?
- Can I read the exact package label?
- Are directions and warnings provided?
- Does the page accurately state the labeled protection terms?
- Are price, stock, delivery and return details current?

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a higher SPF mean I can stay outside longer?
No. Do not use SPF as a time guarantee. Follow the package directions and public-health advice.

### Is “water resistant” the same as waterproof?
No. Check the exact time statement and directions on the product label.

### Does sunscreen replace other sun-protection measures?
No. Follow reliable public-health guidance, which may include seeking shade and wearing protective clothing.

## Conclusion
Compare the exact label rather than relying on a product title or a single number. Product pages should show current package information and avoid claims beyond the label and applicable evidence.
