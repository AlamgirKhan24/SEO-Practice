# Pakistan Market Research — high-level evidence

**Research date:** 21 November 2026. The period represented by each source varies; see caveats. No paid market database was accessed.

## Market indicators and what they do (and do not) show
- DataReportal’s Digital 2026 Pakistan report estimates 117 million internet users in October 2025 (45.6% penetration). This is a broad digital-access indicator, not an ecommerce buyer count. [Source](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-pakistan)
- The U.S. International Trade Administration’s Pakistan ecommerce guide cites local trade reporting of US$5.4 billion ecommerce revenue in 2024 and describes consumer goods and mobile-first commerce. This is a broad-market estimate from secondary reporting, not beauty-specific sales. [Source](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/pakistan-ecommerce)
- Pakistan Bureau of Statistics HIES 2024–25 social indicators reports online purchases by product type, including cosmetics. Its table reports 5% for cosmetics nationally among individuals age 10+ who purchased goods/services online in the last three months. Treat this as survey incidence/category context, not category market share, revenue, or skincare demand. [Source PDF](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/HIES_Report_2024-25-Social-indicators-Reportupdated.pdf)
- SMEDA’s Pakistan Cosmetics Sector 2024 study describes e-commerce as gaining prominence in premium beauty distribution, while modern trade remains significant. Its channel estimates concern premium beauty and should not be generalized to all beauty retail. The report also flags quality/standards and informality issues. [Source PDF](https://smeda.org/index.php?Itemid=1027&download=2172%3Apakistan-cosmetics-sector-2024&id=188%3Asector-studies-reports&option=com_phocadownload&view=category)
- A Karachi-focused premium beauty study describes grey-market and authenticity concerns among the study context. It supports investigating trust, but does not quantify national prevalence. [IBA study](https://ir.iba.edu.pk/sbselp/81/)
- A small, older Abbottabad study reported 3.3% of its participants bought cosmetics online, with most using local shops. Its 2018–19 field period and local sample make it unsuitable as a current national estimate. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10986314/)

## Evidence-based implications
1. Online reach is meaningful, but access figures cannot be converted into demand forecasts.
2. Online cosmetics purchases occur, but the PBS measure is broad and does not isolate skincare; category/page priorities must be validated in Module 03 and with catalog evidence.
3. Trust and product information merit prominence because local research identifies credibility/authenticity as concerns; GlowCare must make only verifiable sourcing statements.
4. Mobile usability is a sensible design priority in a mobile-oriented digital market; validate with the eventual analytics and customer testing.
5. Do not publish market-size projections for GlowCare from broad ecommerce totals or proprietary paid reports that were not accessed.

## Buying behavior hypotheses to test
Customers may compare price, product properties, reviews, brand/seller credibility, and delivery/payment terms before ordering. Current publicly accessible sources reviewed here do not establish a representative national ranking of these factors. Test through interviews, on-site search, support queries, and first-party analytics after launch.

## Delivery and payment considerations
The Trade Administration guide reports cash-on-delivery as a historically important operating consideration based on industry reports; the cited figure is not treated here as a current measured share. Confirm current payment mix and courier coverage with providers and customers. Publish accurate delivery estimates, charges, coverage, payment methods, returns, and damaged/incorrect order processes before launch.

## Challenges and opportunity
- **Authenticity and quality:** verify procurement chain, invoices, batch/expiry controls, and product documentation before making sourcing claims.
- **Market informality / standards:** obtain current regulatory and legal review before selling; this SEO research is not regulatory advice.
- **Price and operational constraints:** model margins, stock updates, delivery costs, failed deliveries, and returns before selecting assortment.
- **Content risk:** skincare claims can imply health/medical effects; require evidence and qualified review, and describe products accurately.
- **Data limitations:** no domain, Search Console, GA4, survey, keyword-volume or paid research data supplied.
