**Site:** [https://www.daraz.pk/](https://www.daraz.pk/)

## 1. Why business analysis matters in SEO

Business analysis establishes what an organization offers, who it serves, how it creates value, and which outcomes matter. SEO can then target searches and pages that support real customer and business journeys. Without this context, an SEO plan may attract irrelevant visits, focus on the wrong page types, or recommend work that does not help shoppers or sellers.

For a marketplace, consider both sides: people looking to buy and businesses or individuals looking to sell. This framing follows Daraz's published marketplace and seller information; it is not a statement about the size of either audience.

## 2. Website overview and business model

### Verified public observations

- The live homepage presents Daraz as an online-shopping destination and links to shopping, seller, support, account and promotional areas. It displays product promotions and category/product discovery content. Product and promotion selections are dynamic and may change between visits. [Daraz homepage](https://www.daraz.pk/)
- Daraz's Terms page explicitly describes the service as an online marketplace and says the site scope includes related subdomains, sites, mobile app, services and tools. It identifies Jade E Services Pakistan Pvt Limited as operator. [Terms and Conditions](https://www.daraz.pk/terms-conditions)
- Daraz's seller page invites sellers to register and use Seller Center to list products through the platform. [Sell on Daraz](https://www.daraz.pk/sell/)
- Homepage copy describes website and app shopping and makes company-published claims about marketplace selection, delivery, payment and vendors. Such statements are Daraz's own positioning, not independently measured facts. [Daraz homepage](https://www.daraz.pk/)

### Business model

The public terms and seller page support describing Daraz.pk as a multi-sided ecommerce marketplace connecting shoppers with marketplace sellers through web/app services. Public pages do not establish revenue mix, current commission rates, fulfillment contracts or seller counts; those details are not asserted here.

## 5. Main business objectives

Daraz's public pages communicate goals such as facilitating shopping, connecting vendors and customers, offering product selection, supporting delivery/payment choices and encouraging seller participation. These are company-stated objectives/positioning; actual commercial targets and their relative importance are not verified.

SEO objectives for this case study to investigate:

1. Help shoppers discover relevant categories and products through non-brand searches.
2. Make priority category, brand and product pages discoverable and understandable to search engines.
3. Support seller acquisition and customer-support journeys where organic search is relevant.
4. Improve qualified organic visits and measurable outcomes if first-party GSC/GA4 data becomes available.

These are strategy hypotheses, not measured Daraz outcomes.

## 6. How users interact with the website

### High-level public journey

1. A visitor lands on the homepage or a search landing page.
2. The homepage offers category/product discovery, promotions, and links to shopping, account and help areas.
3. A shopper may browse categories or follow a product link to product information and the marketplace purchase journey.
4. A seller can follow seller links to read onboarding information and continue toward registration/Seller Center.
5. Buyers can use help, returns/refunds, contact and other customer-care links.

This is a high-level journey inferred from visible links and page purpose. Full checkout, filters, internal search, seller dashboard and account journeys were not tested or documented.

## 8. Organic search opportunities to validate

1. **Category search coverage:** map Pakistani search language to suitable, stable category pages; validate with suggestions, SERPs and keyword tools.
2. **Product discovery:** verify that priority products are reachable through crawlable links and have useful, unique product information. Google recommends crawlable navigation connecting menus, categories, subcategories and products; this is a general guideline to apply in later audits, not a finding that Daraz fails it. [Google ecommerce structure guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/help-google-understand-your-ecommerce-site-structure)
3. **Promotional pages:** investigate whether recurring campaign pages have stable URLs and useful search landing experiences. Indexability and performance are unknown.
4. **Seller information:** explore informational searches about selling online in Pakistan and map relevant intent to seller resources.
5. **Support content:** assess whether help content answers common pre-purchase questions and is internally discoverable.
6. **Blog/category connections:** check whether informational articles link naturally to relevant categories/products.
7. **B2B content:** validate whether corporate purchasing has a distinct search audience before expanding SEO work.

These are opportunity hypotheses, not verified gaps. Keyword/SERP and page evidence is required before prioritization.

## 9. Initial observations

- Public pages indicate that the site serves multiple audience types: shoppers, sellers, buyers needing help, and corporate/bulk purchasers.
- Homepage and category navigation present a broad assortment and promotional discovery paths.
- Public terms and seller information support the marketplace characterization.
- Marketing statements should not be repeated as neutral audit facts or independently verified metrics.
- The homepage is dynamic; date and capture screenshots when recording products, promotions, navigation or category observations.

## 10. Not known from this review

Organic clicks/impressions/CTR/rankings/conversions/revenue; indexed or crawlable URL counts; crawl errors; canonical coverage; structured-data validity; search volumes; backlinks/referring domains; PageSpeed/Core Web Vitals measurements; Daraz's internal category priorities; audience demographics/device mix. These require authorized first-party access or documented public-tool measurements and are not invented here.
